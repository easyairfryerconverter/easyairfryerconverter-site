Why this isn't a simple formula

A microwave primarily heats food by exciting water molecules, while an air fryer uses rapidly circulating hot air. That means microwave minutes cannot be converted directly into air-fryer minutes with a single multiplier.

For the best result, use the converter as a starting range, then check food early and adjust for your specific air fryer.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Quick answers to common questions about this tool and air fryer cooking.

Can microwave time be converted directly to air fryer time?

Not reliably with one multiplier. Microwaves heat food very differently from air fryers, so the useful starting time depends on the food, whether it is frozen or refrigerated, the microwave wattage and whether you want to reheat, cook through or crisp the food.

Does microwave wattage matter when converting to an air fryer?

Yes. A 700W microwave may need longer than a 1000W or 1200W microwave for the same food, so the original microwave time can mean different things. That is why this converter lets you choose the microwave power when you know it.

How do I convert 1 minute in the microwave to an air fryer?

There is no universal 1-minute microwave equivalent. Choose 1 minute in the quick-time buttons, then select the food type and starting state so the tool can give you a more useful air fryer starting range.

Can I use the air fryer instead of the microwave for leftovers?

Often yes, especially when you want to restore crispness to foods such as pizza, fries or breaded items. Start with a moderate time and check frequently so the outside does not overcook before the center is hot.

Can I use this converter for raw chicken or fish?

Use extra care with raw meat and seafood. The converter can provide a starting point, but cooking time alone does not confirm safety. Always check the thickest part for safe doneness with a food thermometer.

Why does the converter give a range instead of one exact time?

Air fryers vary, and food shape, thickness, quantity and starting temperature matter. A range encourages you to check early and stop when the food is heated or cooked correctly instead of relying on a false exact number.
