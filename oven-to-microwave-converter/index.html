FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Quick answers to common questions about this tool and air fryer cooking.

Can oven cooking time be converted directly to microwave time?

Not with a single reliable formula. The microwave result depends on the food, portion size, moisture content and microwave wattage, so this tool gives a conservative starting estimate rather than pretending there is an exact conversion.

Why does microwave wattage matter?

Microwave wattage affects how quickly energy is delivered to the food. A lower-wattage microwave generally needs more time than a higher-wattage model, which is why the converter asks for power when you know it.

Can I microwave any recipe that was written for an oven?

No. Recipes that depend on browning, crisp pastry, dry heat or a set baked structure may not translate well to a microwave. The converter is more useful for reheating, vegetables, potatoes, some proteins and moist dishes.

Will microwave food look the same as oven-cooked food?

Usually not. Microwaves do not brown or crisp surfaces the same way an oven does, so the food can be fully heated while still having a softer or paler exterior.

Should I cover food in the microwave?

That depends on the food. A microwave-safe cover can help retain moisture and reduce splatter for many dishes, but always leave appropriate venting and follow the recipe or appliance guidance when available.

How should I use the estimated time?

Start with the lower end or a shorter interval, check the food, then continue in small increments. This helps avoid overheating and accounts for differences in microwave power and portion size.
